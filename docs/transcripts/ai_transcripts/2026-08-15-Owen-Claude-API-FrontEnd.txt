● Good — staying basketball. Here's my thinking on the two pieces:

  The API question

  You asked me to think hard about this, so here's the reasoning: the brief's
  core requirement is that every statistic trace back to event data, not
  aggregated totals — that's the whole architectural premise already baked into
  our GameEvent/PlayerGameStat schema. That requirement rules out the simplest
  option:

  - balldontlie.io — free, simple REST JSON, Node-friendly, no scraping. But it
  only exposes pre-aggregated box scores, no play-by-play. Using it would mean
  abandoning the event-sourcing story that's central to the brief, not
  fulfilling it.
  - nba_api (Python, wraps stats.nba.com) — free, unofficial but well-maintained
  and widely used in NBA analytics projects, and critically exposes real
  play-by-play endpoints that map directly onto our GameEvent model. This is
  also already the plan noted in PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md's known gaps, so it's not a
  new decision, just confirming it's the right one.
  - Commercial options (Sportradar, SportsData.io) — more reliable, but
  paywalled; not worth it for a capstone project's mock/dev needs.

  Recommendation: nba_api, as already planned — a small Python ingestion script
  writing into the same Postgres DB, with the NestJS API remaining the only
  thing that talks to it over HTTP. That also gives us a natural home for the
  optimizer (see below) without inventing a third architecture pattern.

  The prediction + optimizer core, scoped for basketball

  The FPL proposal's own warning against "80%-done across six subsystems"
  applies double here since it's not even our sport. Translating just the tight,
  demoable core they described:

  1. One prediction model — predicted fantasy points per player for their next
  game (regression over minutes, recent scoring, opponent strength) — the
  basketball analog of "xP."
  2. One optimizer — a salary-cap lineup optimizer (DraftKings/FanDuel-style NBA
  fantasy format is the natural real-world analog): pick the best lineup under
  a budget + positional constraints, solved as MILP (PuLP, in the same Python
  service as the ingestion — reusing the architecture decision rather than
  adding a second new component).

  That's it — no spatial analytics, no Monte Carlo, no multi-model ensemble.
  Small, coherent, finishable.
