"Optimise the chances of winning" could mean two different products:

A predictor — given real data on two teams, output a probability/estimate of who wins (a supervised ML problem, trained on historical results).
A recommender — actually suggest changes (lineup, rotation, strategy) that would increase a team's win probability (a decision/optimisation problem — search over options, not just estimate one fixed outcome).
Your "optimisation engine" branding points toward the second, but "compile analytics... then optimise the chances of winning against real data" reads like the first. They're not the same amount of work — the second needs the first as a building block, plus something to search or compare over.
