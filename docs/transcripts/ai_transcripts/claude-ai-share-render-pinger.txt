Claude responded: A "pinger" for Render is a small script or service that periodically sends HTTP requests to your Render app to keep it awake.

A "pinger" for Render is a small script or service that periodically sends HTTP requests to your Render app to keep it awake.

Why you'd need one

Render's free tier web services spin down after about 15 minutes of no incoming traffic. When a request finally comes in after that, the service has to "cold start" again, which can take 30-60+ seconds. That delay is annoying for demos, APIs your frontend depends on, or anything user-facing.

A pinger just hits your service's URL (often a lightweight /health or /ping endpoint) every few minutes so Render never sees it go idle.

Common ways people implement one

External cron-ping services — things like cron-job.org, UptimeRobot, or Better Uptime let you schedule a GET request to your URL every 5-14 minutes, for free.
GitHub Actions cron job — a workflow with a schedule trigger that curls your endpoint on an interval.
A separate tiny Render cron job / background worker — Render itself supports Cron Jobs as a service type, which you can point at your own web service's URL.
Self-pinging inside the app — a setInterval in your own Node/Python process that pings itself. Works but is a bit hacky and burns a small amount of your own dyno's resources.

Worth knowing

Free-tier "keep-alive via pinging" is a widely used workaround, but Render's own docs don't officially endorse it — it's more of a community trick, and Render could change spin-down behavior at any time.
If your project has real uptime requirements, an upgraded paid instance (which doesn't spin down) is the more robust fix rather than relying on a pinger.

Honest answer: Option 1 (external cron-ping service) is more technically sound, even though Option 2 feels easier to set up. Here's why, then I'll help you get Option 2 working well since that's your preference.

Why Option 1 wins on soundness

|  | External cron service | GitHub/Gitea Actions cron |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reliability of the trigger | Purpose-built for this; runs on schedule reliably | Scheduled workflows are known to be delayed or skipped during high platform load — GitHub explicitly documents that schedule triggers are "best effort," not guaranteed to fire exactly on time |
| Infra dependency | One less moving part | Now your uptime depends on GitHub's/Gitea's scheduler also being up and not throttling you |
| Self-hosted Gitea specifically | N/A | Your Gitea instance needs Actions enabled, a runner registered, and that runner needs to itself stay alive and have network egress to your Render URL — more surface area for something to quietly break |
| Free-tier throttling | Not applicable | GitHub Actions free minutes are generous but scheduled workflows on inactive repos can get disabled automatically after 60 days of repo inactivity |
| Debuggability | Dashboard shows ping history/uptime | You're digging through Action run logs |

The real gap: a cron workflow failing silently (disabled after inactivity, runner down, scheduler delay) means you find out your API is cold-starting during your Milestone 1 demo, not before.

My actual recommendation

Do both, at basically no extra cost:

Set up the external cron-ping service as the primary keep-alive (2 min setup, most reliable).
If you still want the GitHub Actions version for visibility/practice with CI, add it as a secondary pinger — redundancy costs nothing here since Render just sees more traffic either way.

If you want to go with Actions only, it's still a reasonable choice for a student project, not a fireable offense — just go in aware of the "best effort" caveat, and maybe pair it with a manual check before your Milestone 1 demo specifically.

Claude responded: Yes, worth turning on — but with one caveat.

Yes, worth turning on — but with one caveat.

Why it's useful here

If the pinger ever gets a non-200 or an empty body, having the actual response saved (not just the status code) makes debugging way faster — e.g. distinguishing "Render's proxy returned 502" from "my NestJS app threw an error and returned a JSON error body."
Cheap insurance for a student project where you won't be babysitting this daily.

The caveat

cron-job.org's free tier caps how much history it retains (both in number of executions and how long responses are stored — this is worth checking directly on their site since specifics can change, rather than trusting my memory on their exact limits).
Your /health response is tiny ({"status":"ok"}), so storage size isn't a real concern here — this isn't like logging a large payload every 10 minutes.

Recommendation: turn "Save responses" on. Given your response body is minimal and the check runs every 10 min, you're not going to hit any meaningful storage/quota problem, and it gives you an actual paper trail if something breaks before Milestone 1.

One small thing to double check on the settings page: some cron services also let you set a notification on failure (email alert if a ping returns non-200 or times out). If cron-job.org has that option, I'd enable it too — turns "silently broken for 3 days" into "you get an email the moment it fails."

Actually, there is a potential drawback worth flagging before we commit to that.

Render's free tier isn't unlimited compute — it's capped at a set number of instance-hours per month, shared across all your free services. Running the API 24/7 uses roughly the full month's worth of hours on its own (a service up constantly is up ~730 hours in a 30-day month). That confirms the real cap: Render's free tier gives you 750 free instance hours per month that reset each month and don't roll over. Unanswered A month has roughly 720-744 hours in it, so keeping one service alive 24/7 for the whole month uses almost the entire allowance by itself.

That matters for your case specifically:

If the API is your only free service on the account, 24/7 pinging is basically fine — you'll use ~730 of 750 hours, cutting it close but not over.
If your team has other free services on the same Render workspace (frontend static site doesn't count the same way, but a separate ingestion worker or a staging environment would), those draw from the same shared 750-hour pool_. Stack two always-on services and you blow through the cap well before month-end.
What happens if you exceed it: without a payment method on file, Render suspends all your free services for the rest of the month Unanswered — which is strictly worse than the cold-start problem, since it'd mean the API is down entirely, not just slow to wake up, right before Milestone 1.

So it's not "no drawback" — it's "probably fine if the API is the only always-on free service on the account, risky if it's not." Worth a quick check with your team: is anything else on Render under the same workspace right now, or is this API the only free service?

If it's just the one service, I'd say go ahead and let it ping 24/7 — the margin is tight but workable. If there's any doubt, the safer move is limiting the ping schedule to something like 7am-11pm, which keeps you comfortably under budget with a much smaller risk to your account.
