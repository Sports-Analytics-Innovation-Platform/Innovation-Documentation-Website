> 2
> Lineup totals
> Predicted points, salary used and the cap. The salary tile carries a meter, because "$45,100" means nothing until you can see it against the ceiling and the headroom left.
>
> Never colour alone
> The meter turns red past the cap, but the number and the words carry it too — an over-cap lineup says so in text, not just in hue.

> GET /v1/optimizer/lineup
> Lineup.totalSalary / budget
> exists today
> 3
> Lineup table
> The five chosen players with their projection and price, plus a derived dollars-per-point column. That last column is what makes the solver's reasoning visible: it shows why a cheap high-floor player beat an expensive star.
>
> Note
> Dollars per point is computed in the component, not stored — it is a ratio of two fields that are already on the row.
>
> LineupSlot rows
> predictedFantasyPoints, salary
> exists today
> 4
> Solver constraints
> The actual MILP rules — roster size, minimum guards, minimum forwards, the cap, no duplicates — each checked against the current board with a met/unmet state. It turns an opaque optimisation into something you can reason about, and it is what makes local editing safe to offer.
>
> Mirror the solver, do not reinvent it
> The position checks mirror apps/optimizer/optimize.py exactly, including that it substring-matches position, so G-F counts as both a guard and a forward. If the UI and the solver disagree on that, the UI is wrong.
>
> "Editing locally — not saved, resets when you refresh"
> That string is in OptimizerPage.tsx:204 today, and near-identical ones sit in PlayerProfilePage.tsx:186 and GameDetailPage.tsx:184. Three places where the app builds a genuinely useful what-if and then admits it cannot keep it.
>
> A SavedLineup row retires all three. It is also the clearest answer this app has to "why would I make an account?" — because a lineup you tuned is work you did, and losing it costs you something.
>
> Freeze the numbers at save time
> PlayerPrediction is append-and-take-latest: its "current" row moves every time predict.py runs. So a saved lineup has to store salaryAtSave and predictedPointsAtSave on each slot, or re-opening it would silently show different figures than the ones you saved.
>
> Freezing them is also what makes drift computable — "up 4.6 points and $1,200 over cap since you saved it" is only sayable if you kept the original.
